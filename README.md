# Nike Intrinsic Valuation (DCF)

## Business Question
Is Nike’s current market valuation justified under normalized growth, margin recovery, and cost-of-capital assumptions, or does the stock price embed overly optimistic expectations about long-term performance?

## Overview
This project builds a full discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation for Nike to estimate intrinsic equity value and evaluate valuation sensitivity to key assumptions. The model is structured to mirror how valuation work is performed in capital markets, FP&A, and corporate finance roles, with clear separation between historical data, assumptions, operating forecasts, and valuation outputs.

## Methodology
- Analyzed historical financial performance to establish baseline operating trends
- Built a multi-year operating forecast based on revenue growth, margin normalization, and reinvestment assumptions
- Estimated weighted average cost of capital (WACC) using market-based cost of equity and current debt structure
- Valued future free cash flows using a DCF framework with a conservative terminal growth approach
- Benchmarked intrinsic value against public comparables and market pricing

## Key Takeaways
- Nike’s valuation is highly sensitive to terminal operating margins and discount rate assumptions
- Under conservative normalization scenarios, intrinsic value clusters around a narrower range than market pricing implies
- Due to the long duration of cash flows, small changes in WACC have a disproportionate impact on equity value
- Sensitivity analysis highlights where valuation risk is most concentrated
